    07 — Before you ask

    The questions that decide whether we get the order.

    If yours is not here, put it in the message box — an engineer answers it, not a sales script.

    Is the instant price the final price?

    No — and treat any tool that says otherwise with suspicion. It is a reference price built from your geometry and our machine rates. After an engineer reads the drawing it moves. Usually down, because the model has to assume a conservative set-up. Occasionally up, if a feature needs a second set-up or a custom cutter.

    Do you sign an NDA?

    Yes, before you send anything you consider sensitive. Say so in the message box and it comes back countersigned. If a drawing is confidential, send the envelope and the material first — that is enough for a first price, and it identifies nothing.

    What is the smallest order you accept?

    One piece. Prototypes are how most of our export accounts started. The unit price is just higher, because the set-up is carried by one part instead of two hundred — which is exactly what the cost breakdown in the engine is showing you.

    Which files can you actually read?

    STEP and IGES give the cleanest results. STL and OBJ are fine for estimating but carry no tolerance information — they are just a skin. Native SolidWorks, Inventor or DWG files we can also open. A PDF drawing with dimensions is enough if that is all you have.

    How do you handle shipping and terms?

    Ex-works, FOB Shanghai or CIF — your call. Air express for anything urgent, sea for volume. Payment terms start at 30% deposit for new accounts and loosen after a few orders. We are not precious about it.

    Can you take over a part another shop keeps getting wrong?

    That is most of what we do. Send the drawing, the failed parts if you still have them, and the inspection report if there is one. Nine times out of ten the problem is the set-up and the fixturing, not the machine.
